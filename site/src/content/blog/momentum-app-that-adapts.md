## An app that adapts to you, not the other way round

We built Momentum, a fitness app, to show what a [VibeApp](/blog/what-is-a-vibeapp) does better than a traditional app. A VibeApp is an app with a team of AI experts behind it that you simply talk to. And that team shapes the app around your life, instead of asking you to fit into it.

We all know traditional fitness apps. There's a questionnaire at the start, then settings, screens and menus. If your knee starts to hurt, or the machine you need is taken, you have to go back through the screens, the buttons and the settings. It's tedious, it's tiring and it takes time, often for a disappointing result. Some of us simply give up along the way.

**A VibeApp works the other way round. You tell it what's going on, out loud or by typing, and your training plan is updated, tuned by a team of coaches who know you. It's simple, it works, and it's even fun.**

And if you're the one building apps for clients, you know the other side of it too: every one of those screens and settings had to be planned, built and maintained, for situations that users always end up going beyond.

## Just tell it what's happening

With Momentum, everything starts with a sentence, the way it would with a coach who has followed you for weeks. Tell it the machine is taken, and it offers two alternative exercises, shown in 3D, while the rest of your session stays as it was. Tell it you've hurt your shoulder, and it takes upper-body work out of your plan and suggests you see a doctor. Friends coming over on Saturday who are vegetarian? Saturday's dinner becomes vegetarian, and the shopping list follows. And if one day you want more back and shoulder work, it rebuilds Wednesday's session with two new exercises.

Nothing changes behind your back, though. Every change comes to you as a card that shows what will change and why, and you decide whether to accept it.

For whoever builds the app, each of those moments is a branch they never had to design.

## A team of five coaches, each with their own job

Behind Momentum there is a real team. The Head Coach is the one who talks to you. Behind them, four coaches work together, each in their own field: one picks your exercises, one sets your sets, reps and rest, one looks after what you eat, and the last makes sure nothing hurts you, taking your pain and your age into account.

They adapt to your level without you having to set anything. A beginner with a sore knee gets a training plan that is gentle on the knee; someone who already lifts at the gym gets a completely different one, with their own loads. And they follow rules: exercises come from a checked catalogue and are never made up, and every change is reviewed by the coach who watches out for injuries. Ask for four hours of max deadlifts every day with no rest, and nothing is applied: the app offers you a sensible option instead.

Every exercise is shown by an animated 3D figure, with the muscles at work highlighted, so you see the right movement instead of reading a description.

## Behind the app, a control centre: Agentivity

To create Momentum's team, we simply described what we wanted, the way a client would: a team of coaches behind a personal app called Momentum, where the Head Coach is the only one who talks, the Training Coach picks only from a 32-exercise catalogue, the Load Coach sets the dose from the profile, a Nutrition Coach looks after the food, and a Safety Coach checks for injuries.

From those few lines, the Agentivity Assistant built the team, and every fix our tests called for after that was one more sentence to it, never a hand edit.

Building it was the easy part, though. The real work starts once people use the app: every request has to be handled properly, every change has to land without breaking anything, and the cost has to stay where you expect it. That is what **Agentivity** is for. It's the control centre where your agents, your teams and your automations work, and where you watch them and improve them.

During our tests, the Head Coach started showing the person the full notes it sends to the other coaches. In Agentivity we saw, in the execution, which coach was writing what, told the Assistant, and it gave the coaches a mark for their notes to each other, which the app hides: the person now gets one short line instead of the notes. A change like that is made on a draft, and the people using the app see nothing until you publish it.

Everything else is in the same place: every execution and every error, the tokens each coach uses (on the Pro plans and up), the model each one runs on (you choose it per coach: a light one for a simple job, a stronger one where it counts), and who can reach the team, from which app or site. It's the part you would otherwise have to build and maintain yourself around your code, and here it's ready from the first day.

And it isn't only for teams. What Momentum shows, an AI that judges, rules it follows and a person who approves before anything changes, works the same way in an automation: on a hosted plan, an agent can check every morning the invoices that came in, and someone on your team approves them before they're filed, under the same control.

## What if you built one for your clients?

Momentum is about fitness, but yours can be about your client's business.

Picture a client telling you: *"I want an app that adapts to my customers' real lives."* You describe their business to the Assistant, the team takes shape, and you follow it and improve it from Agentivity. You build the app on top, in your client's colours, while the team runs in your workspace, under your control.
