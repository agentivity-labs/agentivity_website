Your people already use AI at work. Some write their emails with it, some summarise documents, some prepare their replies to
customers. Each of them has found their own way, with their own assistant, their own prompts and their own habits. Each of
them gains time, and the company could have the same gain, done its own way.

Instead, it ends up with as many ways of doing the same task as it has people, and it cannot see any of them. Paying for one
assistant for everyone does not change that: each person still writes their own prompts, keeps their own habits and works
alone. The tool is shared, but the way of working is not.

## What shadow AI looks like in an ordinary week

Shadow AI is the use of AI tools at work outside anything the company has chosen or can see. In most cases, nobody is doing
anything wrong. People found a tool that helps, and they use it.

Picture a customer service team on a Monday morning. A customer asks for a refund on an order that arrived late.

The first person on the team opens their assistant and pastes in the customer's message, with the order history, to get a
polite answer. Their prompt, written months ago, says to offer a voucher first. The customer gets a voucher.

The next person handles the same kind of request on Wednesday. Their assistant has never heard of the voucher rule. It writes
a kind, clear answer that accepts the refund straight away.

On Friday, a new team member gets a third case. They have no prompt yet, so they ask a colleague, who forwards theirs by
email. It is an older version.

The same request has three different answers in one week. Nobody chose this, and nobody can see it. The team lead only finds
out when a customer compares notes with a friend.

The same thing happens away from customer service. In HR, two managers write job ads with their own assistants: one sounds
like a startup, the other like a bank, and only one of them remembers the line the company must include in every ad. The
candidates meet two different companies, and nobody notices until someone reads both ads side by side.

Nothing in these scenes is unusual, and nobody behaved badly. Every person used AI to do their job well. The problem is not in
the people: it sits in the way the AI work is organised, or rather, the fact that it is not organised at all.

### Consistency

When each person has their own prompts, the company's process is whatever each person remembers of it. A rule changes, a new
offer starts, a legal sentence must be added: it reaches some assistants and not others. Customers, candidates, suppliers and
colleagues get different answers to the same question, depending on who handled it.

### Control

A personal assistant is personal. Nobody else can see what was asked, what came back, or what was sent on. There is no one
place to check the work, and no clear moment where a person approves before something important goes out.

It also means company information sometimes ends up in personal accounts: a client's message, a contract, a list of prices.
Nobody intended it; it is simply the shortest way to get help.

### Know-how

The best prompts in a company are small pieces of know-how. They hold how your company answers, checks and decides. When they
live in personal accounts, they are not the company's. They are not shared, not improved together, and when the person
leaves, they leave too.

## What it costs on the AI bill

In a company, many tasks are done the same way by many people, again and again. Today, each person often does them in
their own AI chat or with a personal agent, and starts over each time: they write the prompt, paste the document,
explain what they want, then correct the answer and try again. Where AI is billed by use, the company pays for all of that text, and every new turn in a chat sends the whole conversation back to the
model.

With a shared agent, the same task goes through one agent, built once by a professional. Its instructions stay the same
from one run to the next, so the model keeps them in its cache, where reading them costs much less. It follows a few
fixed steps, so nobody rewrites the prompt or starts over.

By our estimate, the usage part of the AI bill for that task falls by about 15% to 43% for a team of 10 who share
it through one agent, by 29% to 55% for 20 people and by 36% to 61% for 50. The saving grows with the number of people,
because the agent runs often enough to keep its cache in use and the cost of building it is shared over more tasks.
Beyond about 50 people, it hardly grows any more: 39% to 63% for 500.

The figure depends most on how often people correct and try again in their chats today, and on how chat apps bill the
earlier part of a conversation, which is not published. Below about ten people, the agent runs too rarely to keep its
cache in use, and the saving can disappear. We count only the AI bill: people's time and seats are not counted, and we
assume the quality of the work is the same on both sides. This is an estimate from simple assumptions, not a measurement.

*How we estimated this:* we modelled one task that many people do, with published
[model prices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) and
[prompt caching rules](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching). We then ran 20,000
combinations of simple assumptions: how much text the task needs, how often people correct and try again, how much they
paste again, how many already have a personal agent, and what it takes a professional to build and maintain the shared
agent. Each range shows the middle 30% of the results. We counted no saving from moving steps to a smaller model.

## Why banning it rarely works

The first reaction is often a rule: no AI tools at work, or only after approval. It is understandable, and it rarely holds.
People use AI because it saves them real effort on real tasks. If the company takes it away and offers nothing in its place,
the use goes on, only less visible than before.

The better answer is to offer something more useful than the personal assistant: AI that already knows how the company works.

## What changes with a shared toolbox of AI teams

Imagine the same customer service team, with one difference. The company has set up an AI team for refund requests, once. It
follows the company's refund process, voucher rule included. Everyone on the team now works from the same AI team, and a
person approves when it matters.

Three things change at once.

**The AI team prepares the work, and your people check it and send it.** The refund answer arrives ready, written the company's
way, and stops where the company wants a human decision. The refund waits for the right person, who approves or refuses it.
Your people keep the decisions and the contact with the customer; the AI does the preparation.

**The same process, whoever does the task.** Monday, Wednesday and Friday get the same answer, because the process lives in
the AI team, not in each person's prompt. When the rule changes, it changes in one place. The two job ads now sound like the
same company, and both carry the line they must.

**An improvement reaches everyone.** When someone finds a better way, the AI team is improved once, and everyone uses the
improved one from their next request. A new team member works the company's way from their first day, with nothing to copy or
forward.

And the work stays in the company's space, where the people you allow can find it, instead of in personal accounts.

## Where to start

You do not need a large project to begin. A good first step is one recurring task that several people do differently today:
a type of customer request, a supplier invoice check, a job ad, a weekly report. Write down how it should be done, have it set
up once as an AI team, by someone on your team or a partner you work with, and give it to everyone who does that task. Then
watch what changes.

Before you choose, three questions help you see where you stand today:

- **If two people on your team handle the same request, do they get the same result?** If you are not sure, the process lives
  in their prompts, not in the company.
- **Could you find, today, the AI work done on a client file last month?** If it sits in personal accounts, you cannot.
- **When your best person leaves, what happens to the way they used AI?** If the answer is "it leaves with them", that know-how
  was never the company's.

None of these questions is about technology. They are about how your company works, and they are worth asking whether or not
you use the Desk.
